Remit-To

Check who should be paid, catch conflicting instructions, and show the evidence before payment.

For the carrier-settlement or AP lead at a small freight brokerage. The demo opens with a June payment redirect that conflicts with a verified March factoring assignment. Remit-To holds the request and shows exactly why.

Target: a 36-hour build with up to four people. Proposed CalHacks / Hyphenate / Spacetime fit and submission timing still require confirmation.

The build decision: finish three credible stories before expanding the world. Begin with a few documents and 36 synthetic development cases. A six-month history is represented by the dated March NOA and June invoice; it does not require simulating every intervening day.

Current state: the repository runs an offline policy demo, tests and scorer. Authenticated reducers, document extraction, the UI and payment-provider integration are still planned.

1. Three stories define the MVP

StoryObserved behaviorEvidence of completion
Clean invoiceMatch a delivered load's USD linehaul invoice to the agreed rate and POD. Prepare payment to the policy payee; a different eligible human approves.Invoice → evidence → decision → request → approval. The live extension shows submitted and settled separately.
DuplicateHold a second invoice against an existing obligation, including a new invoice number or changed requested payee.One load reservation and at most one executable payment request. A concurrent reducer test proves the live guarantee.
RedirectA June request asks to pay the carrier directly while the verified March assignment selects a factor. Hold and ask for release evidence.The factor remains the policy payee; the March NOA and failed R2 check are visible. No automatic release processing.

Keep: evidence detail, human approval, deterministic policy, a small scoreboard, and an honest payment status. Defer: detention/lumper adjudication, general-ledger accounting, full GPS streams, simulated personalities, automatic release handling, free-text scenario generation, and 1,500-load runs.

Outside the supported slice, hold and ask. Do not silently deny charges or short-pay an invoice to make the demo appear autonomous.

2. One owner for business state

Stack: TypeScript throughout; SpacetimeDB for live state, Node for workers, Claude for extraction/explanation, and React/Vite for the UI. Increase sandbox is an optional integration after the recovery contract is verified. Confirm versions before installing dependencies or implementing SDK-specific code.

Start with a board, a load detail view containing documents and rule results, and an approval panel. The scoreboard can be a small summary. Three preset scenario buttons and manual time steps are sufficient.

The draft live records and reducer boundaries are in CONTRACTS.md. The first system has a payment audit trail; a full accounting ledger is a separate feature.

3. Make the payment promise precise

  1. Reserve the obligation atomically. One unresolved or paid obligation per load, independent of payee. Also reserve the normalized carrier/invoice number. Two workers must not independently pass a check and create two payable requests.
  2. Bind approval to an immutable snapshot. Amount, currency, payee, bank destination version and evidence/policy snapshot are approved together. Changes before dispatch invalidate the approval.
  3. Persist the payment identity before sending. Store the provider key and exact payload, then claim the request transactionally. Network calls happen outside reducers.
  4. Keep uncertainty visible. If the provider accepts a transfer and the runner crashes before recording it, restart must reconcile that same key/transfer. A timeout keeps the obligation reserved; it never authorizes a new payment key.
  5. Separate submitted from settled. Provider acceptance means submitted. Only an authoritative terminal success means settled. Duplicate notifications must not duplicate payment events.
  6. Enforce actual identity. The agent cannot approve. An AP lead cannot approve their own request. A different controller may approve an unchanged agent or AP request. Role switching is a local demo convenience using separate seeded identities.

Release gate for the live runner: kill it after provider acceptance but before local acknowledgement, restart it against the same database, and demonstrate one transfer and one recorded outcome. Provider lookup/idempotency semantics must be verified first. The offline tests do not prove this guarantee.

A business refusal must leave business state unchanged and persist an audit event. Do not throw away the transaction and claim the event survived. Test that behavior against the chosen Spacetime version.

Assignment records need trusted verification. The demo's invoice-date rule is a stated policy assumption, not a claim about the legal scope of real factoring agreements. Releases and competing assignments are held for review.

5. Measure the claim you actually tested

The current 36 cases are synthetic development fixtures with repeated parameter variations. They test policy on supplied facts. They are not a blind evaluation, independent document layouts, or a production fraud benchmark.

  • Decision correctness: action, canonical payee, agreed amount and applicable rule must all match an explicit expectation.
  • False holds: report held clean cases divided by all clean cases, with counts.
  • Unsafe requests: report exception cases allowed through and any wrong-payee or wrong-amount requests.
  • Extraction quality: once real document processing exists, report its errors separately from policy errors.

After the three live stories work, freeze policy and ask a teammate to author 6–12 unfamiliar document variants without changing the expected business rules. Keep them out of development. Record who authored and adjudicated them. The extraction worker must never see the answer key or scenario labels.

Label any exposure amount as simulated. Do not claim real dollars saved, production catch rates or payment latency from this offline run. For live latency, include cases still unpaid instead of measuring only successful completions.

7. A 36-hour plan with earlier gates

Use elapsed hours until the actual deadline is confirmed. These are stop/go gates, not a promise that integrations take a fixed number of hours.

  1. H0–2Agree the slice. Assign owners, confirm sponsor fit and tool versions, run the offline core, agree contracts. Confirm the deadline.
  2. H2–6Integrate one decision. One source invoice and POD → validated extraction → reducer decision → evidence view. Use manually supplied extraction only as a visibly labeled fallback.
  3. H6–10Close the approval loop. Clean request, AP self-approval refusal, another controller's approval. Persist one reservation; exercise competing requests.
  4. H10–16Complete the three stories. Clean, duplicate and redirect all work through the same path. Add only enough document variants to expose extraction failures.
  5. H16–22Integrate sandbox payments if ready. Verify provider semantics, distinguish submitted/settled, and pass crash-after-send recovery. If blocked, select the explicit offline mode.
  6. H22–28Challenge the behavior. Run unseen variants, audit evidence and scorer isolation, fix false holds, rehearse ambiguous provider outcomes if integrated.
  7. H28–32Freeze and record. No new features. Score the final build, record the two-minute demo, verify a deterministic reset and fallback.
  8. H32–36Rehearse and submit. Test the judge walkthrough, disclose simulation boundaries, package evidence, and submit ahead of the confirmed deadline.

If H6 slips: stop expanding the world and pair on integration. If H16 slips: cut provider work and all extra UI. Protect the evidence-backed redirect, duplicate guard, approval behavior and honest scoreboard.

8. Show the catch, then show legitimate work

  1. 0:00–0:25 — Redirect. Open the June request. Show the March NOA, required factor payee, hold and question. Explain that the system will not replace verified payment instructions with an unverified request.
  2. 0:25–0:55 — Clean invoice. Show matching invoice/POD and a request approved by a different eligible actor. If integrated, show the actual sandbox status; otherwise label the result simulated.
  3. 0:55–1:15 — Duplicate. Present the same load under a new invoice number. The reservation blocks another payment request.
  4. 1:15–1:40 — Approval control. AP creates a request and tries to approve it: refused. A different controller approves. Separately, an agent approval attempt is refused by role.
  5. 1:40–2:00 — Evidence. Show counts, false holds and unsafe requests. State which components are real, which are simulated, and which have not been implemented.

For a live walkthrough, let a judge switch between seeded demo personas and inspect the source evidence. Offer preset clean, duplicate and redirect scenarios. An unfamiliar currency or charge should produce a clear hold, not invented policy.

Do not demonstrate a controller being refused merely because the agent requested payment. That contradicts the intended separation of duties. The requester and approver must be different people/identities with appropriate permissions.

10. Validate the business and policy next

  • Buyer: ask brokerage AP staff for a recent redirect or duplicate exception, its frequency, handling time and required sign-off. Determine where invoices and verified assignments currently live.
  • Domain policy: challenge assignment scope, release requirements, disputed amounts and callback verification with someone who handles carrier settlements. Capture agreed demo assumptions in the contracts.
  • Adoption: identify the smallest integration that lets an AP lead inspect a hold and approve a legitimate request. An autonomous-seat pitch is premature until exception handling and trust are demonstrated.
  • Event fit: confirm sponsor requirements, track eligibility and the submission deadline. The plan does not assume those have been checked.
  • Technical prerequisites: confirm local Spacetime/SDK support and the payment provider's recovery guarantees before enabling those paths.

The next commercial milestone is a useful review of anonymized real exceptions, with permission. A larger synthetic world is not a substitute for learning whether the user would trust and adopt the workflow.
